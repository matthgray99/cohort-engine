# Copywriting Standards

## The Standard
All copy must be Claude Hopkins + David Ogilvy + Russell Brunson + Alex Hormozi caliber, written in Matt Gray's voice.

## Matt Gray's Voice
- Builder language: "build", "install", "architect", "deploy", "stack", "engine", "machine"
- Systems thinking: frames everything as architecture, not hustle
- Direct and honest: no fluff, no motivation-speak, no filler
- "Let's get after it" energy
- Empathy without softness: believes in people past their limitations
- Specific over generic: names the number, the timeline, the exact transformation
- No em dashes (use -- or reword)

## Principles by Master

### Claude Hopkins (Scientific Advertising)
- Specific claims beat vague promises
- Test headlines relentlessly
- Reason-why copy: always explain WHY something works
- One clear proposition per piece

### David Ogilvy
- Every word earns its place. If it doesn't advance, cut it.
- "You can't bore people into buying"
- Long copy sells when it's interesting
- Research first, write second
- Headlines do 80% of the work

### Russell Brunson (Expert Secrets / DotCom Secrets)
- Story-driven: bridge from current state to desired state
- Stack the value (show what they'd pay individually vs what they get)
- Future pacing: paint the picture of life after implementation
- Epiphany bridge: one story that changes their belief

### Alex Hormozi (Grand Slam Offer)
- Perceived value must exceed price by 10X
- Remove all risk from the buyer
- Stack deliverables with specific outcomes
- Time to value matters: faster results = more valuable

## Email Rules
- Subject lines: specific, curiosity-loaded, benefit-driven. Hopkins-level.
- Every sentence earns the next sentence
- ONE primary CTA per email
- Emotional specificity: name the feeling, not just the problem
- P.S. lines are the second-most-read part after the subject line

## Deck Copy Rules
- 1-2 sentences per slide MAX
- Billboard slides: ONE sentence that could be a poster
- Framework names should be memorable and ownable
- Case studies: specific numbers, specific timelines, specific transformations
- Never generic ("grow your business") -- always specific ("compress 5 offers to 1 and double your margin")
